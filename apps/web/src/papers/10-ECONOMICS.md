## 1. The principle, and why it is the first section

Maschina earns from work performed. It does not earn from outcomes, and it never claims them.

This is a structural decision rather than a modest one. The evidence on active trading is consistent
across decades and jurisdictions: ninety seven percent of persistent day traders in the largest study of
the question lost money net of fees, with fewer than one in a hundred earning more than a bank teller
[3]. The most active traders in a long study of brokerage accounts underperformed the market by more
than six points annually, and under one percent of day traders in a fifteen year study were reliably
profitable [4].

A business that only earns when its users profit will eventually need its users to believe they are
profiting. That single pressure produces most of the dishonesty this industry is known for. Charging for
work removes the pressure entirely, and it is why every result in this product is displayed beside the
benchmark of having done nothing at all. Maschina never takes a share of a machine's profits.

## 2. Revenue

| Source | Basis |
| --- | --- |
| Execution fees | A percentage of each transaction, shown before a machine starts, with faster paths priced above scheduled ones |
| Copy revenue | A share of the fee on copied machines, the remainder to the creator |
| Plans, if introduced | A monthly price in exchange for lower fees or higher limits, never required |
| Network share | A portion of what the network earns selling compute, bandwidth and execution |
| Issuance fees | A fee on assets issued through the platform |
| Machine payments | A fee on payments machines make to services and to each other |
| Idle yield share | A portion of yield earned on balances that would otherwise sit idle |

Rates are published on the pricing page rather than here, so they are never authoritative in two places
at once.

## 3. Where value goes

Owners keep what their machines earn, net of fees. Creators earn for as
long as copies of their machines run. Node operators earn for verified work at rates set by scheduling.
Reporters earn bounties for defects and for assets the checks missed. Maschina takes a share of
activity.

## 4. Credits, and the question of a token

Maschina has no token.

An internal credit unit is the appropriate instrument first: earned by operators for capacity, spent by
owners on execution and priority, denominated in work that actually occurred.

If a token is ever issued, three commitments made in advance govern it. It follows real usage rather
than preceding it. It buys things, being compute, priority, fee discounts and bonds, rather than
representing a share of profits. And it is not a fundraising mechanism presented as a product.

Maschina will not operate its own chain. Solana provides throughput, cost, speed and the breadth of
assets reachable from it, and a chain of our own would add a decade of work to solve a problem we do not
have [5].

## 5. The numbers that decide this

**Fee per active machine per month**, which follows from how often a machine acts rather than how much
it holds. **Machines per owner**, which is the difference between a tool and a platform. **Copy revenue
per published machine**, which is where the marketplace becomes a business rather than a feature. **Cost
per machine hour**, which falls sharply once execution moves onto contributed hardware.

The first commercial milestone is deliberately unglamorous: the founder's own machines covering the
platform's running costs. It requires no other participant, it is measurable, and a system
that cannot clear it has no business asking anyone else for money.

## 6. The moat

Not strategies, which anyone can copy. Not models, which are commodities. Not compute, which is
purchased.

The moat is being the place where software can be trusted with money, and it has three components: the
custody design, the record that makes behaviour provable, and the standing that accumulates on top of
it. All three strengthen with use. None can be forked, because a record with no history is worth
nothing, and history is the one asset that cannot be copied.

## 7. What would break this

**Fee compression.** Competitors arrive and execution fees fall toward zero. Our fee is for safety and
execution quality rather than routing, and both are measured and published, which is defensible but not
indefinitely.

**Regulation.** Parts of the surface become unavailable in significant markets. Trading a person's own
funds on their own instruction is by far the lightest case, which is one reason it is first. The rest
arrive with a structure or not at all.

**Custody concentration.** Being the safest place to hold machine funds makes Maschina the most valuable
target in the category. Security spending scales with success permanently, and is treated as a cost of
goods rather than as overhead.

**Machines not performing.** If funded machines do not beat doing nothing, owners stop funding them.
There is no mitigation except measuring honestly, publishing the benchmark beside every result, and
letting people decide with real information. Every other response to this risk is a lie with a delay
on it.
