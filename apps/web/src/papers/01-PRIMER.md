## The problem, in one paragraph

Give an automated trader your money and you are relying on it to restrain itself. Its limits live
in its own configuration and are enforced by its own code. That arrangement holds until the first
unexpected market, the first defect, the first stolen key. Then it fails completely, and it fails in the
only way that matters, which is that the money is gone and does not come back.

This is not a small technical complaint. In 2024, roughly forty-four percent of stolen funds came from
attackers obtaining private keys rather than from any flaw in a protocol or a contract. The weak point
is custody, and custody is exactly what every agent framework hands to the agent.

## What Maschina does about it

A machine on Maschina holds funds in a wallet created for it, whose policy is enforced by a key
management provider that knows nothing about Maschina's budgets or rules, and permits only approved
programs, assets and recipients, and never exports the key. It spends against a budget held in a ledger
it cannot edit. Its actions are checked by a rule engine that does not hold its signing key. It runs on
nodes that never hold a private key. Everything it decides, and why, is appended to a record that can
only be added to.

Independent checks must agree before anything is signed, and any one of them can refuse alone. The last
is enforced by the wallet provider, outside our infrastructure.

## What that makes possible

Once software can hold money safely, it can do work. Trading first, because it is the hardest test
there is: real money, live markets, continuous operation, and an immediate cost to being slow.

Then the rest. Machines that hold positions and close them. Machines that pay for data, compute and
each other's work. Machines that hire other machines with budgets tighter than their own. Machines
published by the people who built them and copied by people who want them. All of it running on a
network of computers owned by other people, who are paid for the work they do.

## Why now

The x402 payment standard for software agents arrived in 2026, carrying Visa, Stripe, Google, AWS,
Coinbase and the Solana Foundation into a single foundation in April. As of 2026, Solana settles
roughly half to two thirds of all x402 payment traffic. At the time of writing, the rails are being laid
faster than the safety is.

The gap is the opportunity, and it is a gap in architecture rather than in intelligence.

## What we will never do

We will not hold your money. We will not promise returns, publish numbers that did not happen, or show
a projection dressed as an expectation. Most automated trading loses money over time and we say so in
our own documents, because a business that needs its users to believe they are winning eventually needs
them to be wrong about it.
